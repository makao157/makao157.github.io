Wells Fargo · Case study

Clarity at the moment of waiting.

A credit card application does not end with the form. The moments after submission need just as much care. My work focused on helping applicants understand their status, recognize their application, and know what happens next.

01

The challenge

After submitting an application, people need answers: Has it been received? Is a decision still pending? What should I expect next? The application-status experience needed a clearer hierarchy and more useful guidance at a sensitive moment.

02

My contribution

I worked hands-on with a content strategist to keep the interface and messaging aligned with Wells Fargo’s brand. My design work covered mobile and desktop application-status screens, next-step guidance, and related balance-transfer and thank-you experiences.

03

From journey to interface

Journey mapping and a current-versus-new screen audit helped frame the application experience. The design process moved through draft concepts, working designs, and delivery designs, with attention to the relationship between status, application details, and follow-up information.

04

Three things applicants need

The visual hierarchy puts application status first, reference details second, and next-step guidance close to the main message. Pending and declined states need distinct language; neither should imply approval. The same information order carries across mobile and desktop.

05

A considered handoff

Related flows included a balance-transfer confirmation modal and an application thank-you page. Clear choices, recognizable reference details, and a reminder to sign off help users understand where they are and how to leave the experience.

06

What this work taught me

The empty moments between actions carry real weight in a financial product. Pairing interface design with content strategy helps make those moments more understandable. The portfolio refresh shown here is a conceptual presentation of historical work; no measured performance improvement is claimed.
