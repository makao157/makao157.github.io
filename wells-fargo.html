Wells Fargo · Case study

Clarity at the moment of waiting.

A credit card application does not end with the form. The moments after submission need just as much care. My work focused on helping applicants understand their status, recognize their application, and know what happens next.

01

Overview

For Wells Fargo’s Active Cash Card, I redesigned the application-status experience across mobile and desktop, including pending and decline decision screens, the balance-transfer flow, and the thank-you page. The goal was for applicants to know their status, reference details, and exactly what happens next.

02

The problem

After submitting an application, people still needed answers: What happens now? When will I hear back? Is there anything I need to do? The existing reviewing-your-application experience left applicants guessing at a moment when the bank needed to build trust.

03

My role & timeline

Senior Lead Product Designer, January–October 2022. I owned the application-status redesign and worked hands-on with a content strategist to keep interface design and messaging within Wells Fargo’s brand. Reviews moved from draft concept to working design to delivery design, targeting a September release with final design delivered in October 2022.

04

Research & discovery

Journey mapping covered six stages: Prepare; Scope & Frame; Map Current State; Map Aspirational State; Map Target State; Deliver & Support. A current-versus-new audit compared pending screens side by side to identify where applicant confidence broke down.

05

Application status: mobile and desktop

The redesigned mobile review screen gives applicants a clear Pending status, an application reference number, and What’s next guidance covering email confirmation. A reassuring sign-off closes the message. Status, reference details, and next steps form a consistent information hierarchy across devices.

06

Balance transfer & thank-you experience

The desktop balance-transfer flow included a Did you change your mind? confirmation modal, making it easier to proceed or step back. The application thank-you page brought together status, reference details, next-step bullets, and a security reminder to sign off before leaving.

07

Outcome & takeaways

The original case study describes delivery of a consistent application-status experience across mobile and desktop. Applicants see their status, reference number, and next steps together. The main lesson: the moments between actions carry as much UX weight as the actions themselves. Designing the wait is part of designing the product.
